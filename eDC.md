## 1. About eDC IIT Delhi

The Entrepreneurship Development Cell (eDC), IIT Delhi is one of the most active student-run organisations on campus. Founded in 2007, eDC’s mission is to build entrepreneurial thinking among students — through events, mentorship, resources, competitions, and community.

The cell operates through **verticals** — focused teams that own specific functions. The Technology Vertical is responsible for all things technical: the organisation’s digital infrastructure, products, and initiatives that sit at the intersection of technology and eDC’s broader mission.

---

## 2. Background: The eDC Knowledge Base

A few years ago, the Technology Vertical built a resource portal for the eDC community, hosted on Notion:
[edciitd.notion.site](https://edciitd.notion.site)

The idea behind it was simple and genuinely useful: give any IIT Delhi student exploring entrepreneurship a single place to find what they need: books, reports, funding opportunities, courses, mentorship, department connections.

### 2.1. What it currently contains
The portal is organised into three main sections:
*   **LEARN**: Curated books (*The Lean Startup*, *Zero to One*, etc.), podcasts, courses (*Venture Studio*, *VentureVerse*), and a large library of industry reports from Blume, Kalaari, and others.
*   **STARTUP (Make Your Startup)**: Competitions, departmental facilities, funding schemes, and important institutional connects (Makerspace, i-TTO, R&D Partnerships).
*   **RAISE**: Funding database (IHFC, IDEX, BIRAC, Atal Incubation, YC/Antler/Build3 cohorts, VC firms), pitch resources, investor email templates, and cohort programs.

It also features a mentorship booking system and an eDC Weekly newsletter embed.

### 2.2. Why it is no longer working
The portal exists. But nobody opens it voluntarily. Here is an honest assessment:
*   **The content is outdated** — reports are from 2022–23, several sections have placeholder content (*Page 1, Page 2, Page 3...* with no actual material)
*   **There is no reason to return** — nothing is dynamic, nothing signals freshness
*   **Discovery is poor** — you need to know what you are looking for to find it
*   **The design is generic Notion gallery** — no personality, no identity
*   **It does not feel like a product.** It feels like a folder.

The intent was right. The execution needs a complete rethink.

## 3. Your Task

> **Reimagine the eDC Knowledge Base from scratch.**
>
> The existing portal gives you a sense of *what* it was trying to do. Your job is to figure out what it *should* be, and then build toward that.
>
> You are not being asked to update a Notion page. You are being asked to think like a product person: who is the user, what do they actually need, what would make them come back, and what does a version of this that is genuinely useful look like?

### 3.1. Constraints and Freedoms
*   You can use **any technology**: a website, a web app, Notion, a no-code tool, whatever you think is the right medium for what you are building.
*   You can use **AI tools, vibe coding tools, or any other assistant** to help you build; we will evaluate the *output and the thinking*, not whether you typed every line yourself.
*   You can **completely rethink the structure**; LEARN/STARTUP/RAISE is not sacred. If you think the information should be organised differently, go ahead.
*   The **content domain stays the same**: entrepreneurship resources for IIT Delhi students (learning materials, startup tools, funding opportunities, mentorship, community).

### 3.2. What We Are Evaluating
We are not looking for the most technically complex submission. We are looking for submissions that demonstrate:
1.  **Product thinking**: Did you understand the real problem? Did you make deliberate decisions about structure, user flow, and what to include?
2.  **Execution quality**: Is what you built actually good? Does it feel better than what exists?
3.  **Ambition**: Did you push yourself beyond the obvious answer?

---

## 4. Submission Format

Build a prototype of your idea and submit a supporting document that explains your thought process, including the problem being solved, target users, key assumptions, design decisions, feature prioritization, and overall approach. The goal is not only to demonstrate what you built, but also to show how you arrived at those decisions and how the product evolves from concept to execution.

> **Note on cumulative submission**: A fully working prototype is evaluated as a complete package: your thinking (PRD-level rationale), your execution (MVP quality), and your polish (prototype). You do not need to submit separate documents for each tier. A single well-documented build with a brief write-up explaining your decisions is sufficient.

---

## 5. A Note Before You Begin

The best submissions we have seen in past recruitment cycles were not the most technically impressive. They were the ones where it was obvious the person had actually *thought* about the user, where the structure, the content choices, and the design decisions all reflected a point of view about what would be genuinely useful to a student at IIT Delhi trying to figure out entrepreneurship.

The existing Knowledge Base was built by people with good intentions. Your job is not to criticise it, it is to make something better. The bar you are competing against is not the Notion portal. The bar is: ***would someone actually want to use this?***

That is the only question that matters.
